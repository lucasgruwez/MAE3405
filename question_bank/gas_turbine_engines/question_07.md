# Question 07 — Turbojet afterburning

Source: [Topics 9–10 problem set](../../problem_sets/MAE3405%20Problem%20Set%20Questions%209-10.pdf), Q22(i–iii), PDF p. 2 (printed p. 12 of 16), referring to Q21 on PDF p. 1.

## Question

The exhaust gases in the jet pipe of the engine below are reheated to 2000 K by supply of Jet A1 fuel to an afterburner; the combustion pressure loss incurred is 3% of the pressure at the outlet from the turbine. The intake air mass flow rate and gas-generator operating point remain unchanged. Use a constant-property combustion model and 98% afterburner efficiency. Both dry and reheated propelling nozzles are converging.

| Quantity | Value | Quantity | Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compressor Ratio | 9.0 | Compressor Cp | 1.005 kJ/kg.K |
| Turbine Inlet Temp | 1250 K | Compressor γ | 1.4 |
| Isentropic Efficiencies | | Turbine Cp | 1.148 kJ/kg.K |
| Compressor | 87% | Turbine γ | 1.333 |
| Turbine | 87% | Exhaust Gas Constant R | 0.287 kJ/kg.K |
| Intake | 95% | Engine air Mass Flow | 15 kg/s |
| Propelling Nozzle | 95% | Airspeed | 260 m/s |
| Mechanical Efficiency | 99% | Altitude | 7000 m |
| Combustion Efficiency | 98% | Combustor Pressure drop | 6% |

Calculate:

(i) the percentage increase in nozzle area required,

(ii) the percentage increase in net thrust,

(iii) the percentage increase in fuel flow rate & TSFC.
