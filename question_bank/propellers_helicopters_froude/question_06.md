# Question 06 — Helicopter fuel use, BSFC and thermal efficiency

Source: [Topics 5–6 problem set, Question 15*, printed p. 7 / PDF p. 2](../../problem_sets/MAE3405%20Problem%20Set%20Questions%20Week%205-6.pdf#page=2).

**Question 15*.** A helicopter of mass 750kg hovers at 1000m. Its main rotor has two blades, each of length 4m. Its tail rotor also has two blades, each of length 0.75m. The power to drive the tail rotor is 10% of that required to drive the main rotor. The SI four-stroke engine specifications are:

| Quantity | Value | Quantity | Value |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Bore | 0.1m | Combustion efficiency | 98% |
| Stroke | 0.12m | Mechanical efficiency | 90% |
| RPM | 2500 | Volumetric efficiency | 85% |
| Cylinders | 6 | Air to fuel ratio | Stoichiometric |
| Fuel approximated by | CH₂.₂ | Fuel LHV | Avgas |
| $c_p$ (fuel & air) | 1.055kJ/(kg K) | $c_p$ (products) | 1.486kJ/(kg K) |
| $c_v$ (fuel & air) | 0.768kJ/(kg K) | $c_v$ (products) | 1.199kJ/(kg K) |

Blade lengths are centre-to-tip radii. Volumetric efficiency refers to air induction at ambient conditions, with stoichiometric fuelling of all induced air. Use atomic masses C=12, H=1, O=16 and N=14 kg/kmol.

Treat hovering as a static-thrust condition and determine:

(i) volumetric fuel consumption per hour;

(ii) brake specific fuel consumption in kg/(kW h);

(iii) the thermal efficiency of the engine.
