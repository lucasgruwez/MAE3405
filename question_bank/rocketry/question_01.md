# Question 01: Single-stage rocket and propellant comparison

Source: [Weeks 11–12 problem set](../../problem_sets/MAE3405%20Problem%20Set%20Questions%20Week%2011-12.pdf), Question 29, PDF page 1 (printed page 15).

A single-stage rocket launches a 50 kg satellite. Its structure has mass 1500 kg. It starts from rest at sea level and burns for 50 s at a constant flight-path angle of 80° above horizontal. The chamber stagnation pressure is 3.0 MPa. An ideal converging-diverging nozzle has throat area 0.100 m² and expands the exhaust to the atmospheric pressure at 15 km.

Compare kerosene/O₂ and H₂/O₂ propellants. Use an exhaust specific heat ratio of 1.15 for both combinations. Their effective bulk liquid propellant densities are 1070 kg/m³ and 264 kg/m³ respectively; neglect tank ullage.

During powered flight, assume constant chamber conditions, propellant flow and gravity. Neglect aerodynamic drag and off-design pressure thrust, and take the effective exhaust velocity as the nozzle exit velocity. For coasting, use an inverse-square-gravity radial model with the vertical component of burnout velocity, neglecting its horizontal component.

For **each** propellant combination, calculate:

(a) The nozzle exit velocity.

(b) The propellant mass flow rate.

(c) The thrust at the design altitude of 15 km.

(d) The specific impulse at that altitude.

(e) The total propellant mass and liquid storage volume.

(f) The speed increment at burnout, including gravity loss.

(g) The vertical burnout height and maximum coast altitude above sea level.
