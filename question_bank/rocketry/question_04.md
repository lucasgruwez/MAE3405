# Question 04: Hydrogen–oxygen rocket nozzle comparison

Source: [Weeks 11–12 problem set](../../problem_sets/MAE3405%20Problem%20Set%20Questions%20Week%2011-12.pdf), Question 33, PDF page 2 (printed page 16).

A rocket motor burns a stoichiometric mixture of hydrogen and oxygen. Both reactants enter at −23°C. Combustion takes place at chamber stagnation pressure 7.0 MPa with combustion efficiency 90%. The magnitude of the water-vapour formation enthalpy is 241.827 MJ/kmol.

The motor uses a combustion tap-off cycle for its turbopumps. Neglect the tapped mass and its separate thrust contribution. Assume complete reaction to water vapour, constant species heat capacities and no dissociation. Apply combustion efficiency to chemical heat release.

At an altitude of 20,000 m, determine:

(i) The thrust from a converging nozzle with exit diameter 0.500 m and isentropic efficiency 95%.

(ii) Whether replacing it with a converging-diverging nozzle increases the thrust. Use the same throat area, the same choked mass flow and the same 95% nozzle efficiency, and expand the exhaust to ambient pressure. Calculate the new thrust and percentage increase.
